## What's New in Version 1.2.1

Division standings now come straight from ESPN on both apps, and a team leading its division is said
as "Leader" instead of a dash your screen reader skipped.

### Windows

**Standings**
- **Games back and order come from ESPN.** MLB, NFL, NBA and NHL standings use ESPN's own divisions,
  its order (which applies each league's tiebreakers to teams level on record) and its games back.
  Figures read as ESPN writes them: "6", not "6.0".
- **A leader's games back reads "Leader".** It was a dash, which JAWS and NVDA don't speak at their
  usual punctuation setting, so a leader's games back was silent. Teams level at the top are both
  leaders. In college standings it reads "0", since college games back counts from the conference's
  top seed. A game's own Standings tab follows the same rule.
- **NBA and NHL division tables say their conference** as you arrive ("Central, Western Conference,
  Standings"), since both leagues have a Central and a Pacific. Their tabs are named by division
  alone: "Atlantic", not "Eastern Conference Atlantic".
- **MLB wild card:** a division leader's position reads "Leader" and its wild card games back "N/A",
  and a team outside the playoff spots reads "Not in a playoff spot" instead of a dash.
- No team can drop out of its division because ESPN changed its abbreviation.

### iPhone: Standings

- **Games back is counted from the division leader.** The NFL, NBA and NHL standings showed games
  back from the conference leader under each division's heading, so a team leading its division
  could read as behind: Denver showed 0.5 back while leading the AFC West. Divisions, their order and
  their games back now come from ESPN, and teams level on record are in ESPN's order, which applies
  each league's tiebreakers, rather than an order that could change from one look to the next.
- **A leader's games back reads "Leader"** instead of a dash VoiceOver skipped. In college standings
  it reads "0", since college games back counts from the conference's top seed. A game's own
  Standings section does the same.
- **NBA records** show in full (such as 8-3) in every row.
- **MLB wild card:** a division leader's games back reads "N/A", and the team at the cut line "0".
- **CFL standings** read "N/A" for games back, which the CFL's data doesn't have, instead of a dash.
