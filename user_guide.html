Getting Started

Run Scores from the Start menu (or Scores.exe if you use the portable download). The app opens to a list of sports leagues. Move through the list with the ↑ and ↓ arrow keys and press Enter to open a sport. Press Escape or Alt+B to go back.

Keeping Scores Up to Date

Scores checks for a new version each time it starts and tells you only when one is available. Answer Yes and it downloads the update and installs it for you; Scores closes while the installer runs, then you can start it again. Nothing is installed without asking.

To check whenever you like, choose Check for Updates at the bottom of the home screen list. It always tells you the result, including when you are already up to date. To stop the check at startup, clear Automatically check for updates at startup in Settings.

Navigation

The same basic pattern applies everywhere in the app:

  • ↑ / ↓ — move through a list
  • Enter — open the selected item
  • Escape or Alt+B — go back to the previous screen
  • Tab / Shift+Tab — move between controls such as buttons, tabs, and dropdowns

When a screen has tabs, Tab moves to the tab bar and the arrow keys move between tabs.

Home Screen

The home screen shows three things:

  • Live Scores – All Sports — all games in progress or completed today, across every sport
  • Your list of sports leagues (controlled by the Settings button)
  • Favorite team cards at the top, if you have added any

Scores and Game Details

Opening a sport shows that day's games. Arrow keys move between them. Press Enter on a game to open its details — score, venue, weather, broadcast info, and more.

To view games from a different date:

  • Alt+P — previous day
  • Alt+N — next day
  • Ctrl+G — jump to a specific date

From game details you can open additional views:

  • Box score — player and team statistics
  • Plays (MLB) — play-by-play, inning by inning
  • Plays (basketball, hockey) — play-by-play by period, with spatial audio (see below)
  • Drives (NFL) — drive-by-drive breakdown by quarter
  • Standings — current league standings
  • Leaders — top statistical performers
  • Injuries — injury reports for both teams
  • News — recent headlines for teams in the game

Who is on the ice (NHL)

While an NHL game is being played, its details have an On the ice row just after the game time. It reads both teams' players by last name, away team first, for example "On the ice: Rangers: Zibanejad, Lafreniere, ...; Kraken: Kakko, ...". Press Enter on it for a list of each team, then its players: forwards, then defense, then the goalie, each with a jersey number and position. Each team's heading says how many are on the ice; fewer than six usually means a penalty or a pulled goalie. The list checks for the latest as it opens, and says "On the ice changed" if anyone has. Press F5 in the list to check again; you stay on the same player if he's still on the ice. Escape closes it. The row isn't there before or after a game, in an intermission, or for college hockey, which ESPN doesn't send.

Listening to a game on the radio

For the NFL, NBA, NHL, and college football and basketball, Scores knows where to hear a game. On a game in the scores list or Live Scores, press Alt+L, or open the context menu (Shift+F10 or the Applications key) and choose Listen to the Game. A menu lists the places to listen, nearest first: the away team's radio, the home team's, and then the services that carry every game, such as SiriusXM. Choosing one opens it in your web browser. If the service asks you to sign in or subscribe, that happens there; Scores doesn't play the audio itself.

Game details have the same menu, as a Listen to the Game row and the Listen button (Alt+L). A team's Team Hub has Listen on the Radio (Alt+L). Each of these leagues' lists ends with Game Radio: every team with its flagship station, and the services that carry every game. College lists the schools of the SEC, Big Ten, ACC and Big 12, and Notre Dame. A school's radio network carries its football and basketball.

Stations and their pages change. The links are checked every night, and one that stops working is fixed in the next update.

Monitoring games

Live Scores starts with a Monitoring section, for the games you want to keep up with. On a game in Live Scores, press Alt+M to add it. It stays in its own section, which says it's monitored, and a copy appears under Monitoring with its sport at the end, so you can go through just your games. They're in the order you added them, and Enter opens one as it does anywhere else.

You can also press Alt+M on one of today's games on a sport's page. It's added to Live Scores' Monitoring section, which is the only place it shows. Only today's games can be monitored, since Live Scores shows today.

To stop monitoring a game, press Alt+M on it again, in either place. Done on a Monitoring row, it moves you to the next game there, or to the section's heading if there's none after it. Monitoring lasts until you quit Scores, wherever you go in the meantime. Scores doesn't read out score changes: the Monitoring section is where to find them.

Hearing where a play happened

In baseball, Alt+P on a pitch plays a tone for where it crossed the plate. Basketball and hockey work the same way, using the shot and play locations ESPN reports. A tone's note says how far from the basket or the goal the play was — close in is a high note — and its left-right position says where across the court or the ice. Each play reads its spot as part of its entry in the list, for example “from three, left side”, “near end, in the slot” or “far end, behind the net”. In hockey, left and right are as you face the goal at that end, so you hear where it happened as you move through the plays. Nothing is spoken over the tone itself.

  • Alt+P — play the selected play's location
  • Alt+S — play every located play in the period, in order. Pressing Alt+P or Alt+S again cuts it off and plays the new choice; closing the plays window stops it.
  • Shift+F10 — the same options as a menu

Not every play has a location. Substitutions, timeouts, free throws and most face-offs arrive without one, so about a third of basketball plays and one in twenty hockey plays are silent. The period heading says how many can be heard, and a play with no location says so rather than playing a tone that would be a guess.

Table Views

Wherever statistics appear in a table, you can switch between three display formats. Quick List and Full List work well with screen readers. Table view works well visually.

Press Tab to move between the tabs and a table, or F6 where a dialog has tabs. The keys below work from the tab bar as well as inside the table, and the view you choose is used for the next table you open. A box score has a tab for each team's players or stat group and a Team Stats tab comparing both teams.

Where there would be more than eight tabs, there is a list instead, as with college conferences in Standings and Teams and the tables in a football box score. Up and Down go round past the ends, and Home, End and typing work as they do on tabs. Press Enter on the one you want to open its table, and Escape or the Back button to come back to the list on the same row. Ctrl+Tab goes from one table to the next, and the keys below work once a table is open. Player and Team Statistics work the same way, with a list of the statistics. In either, Alt+D lists ESPN's definition of each statistic on the screen, the same wording the iPhone shows. A statistic ESPN doesn't define says "No definition from ESPN."

On any tab bar, Left and Right go round past the ends, and Home and End go to the first and last tab. Type the start of a tab's name to go to it: one letter goes to the next tab starting with that letter, the same letter again goes on to the one after, and different letters typed quickly narrow it down, so S, U, N reaches Sun Belt.

Sports and Hubs

Standard leagues

MLB, NFL, NBA, NHL, WNBA, NCAAF, NCAAM, NCAAWB, NCAAH, NCAAWH, and Soccer all open a games list for the current day.

A soccer league's list ends with Standings, Statistics and Teams. Standings has position, games played, wins, draws, losses, goals, points and where a place leads. A competition with no table, such as the CONCACAF Champions Cup, says so, and its Teams still lists the teams. Statistics goes straight to the player leaders, since ESPN has no team statistics for soccer.

The college leagues' lists have Polls, with a tab for each poll ESPN publishes, in ESPN's order. College football has six: the College Football Playoff committee's rankings, the AP Poll, the AFCA Coaches Poll, the FCS Coaches Poll, and the AFCA Division II and Division III polls. Basketball has the AP and Coaches polls, and hockey the USCHO poll. Ctrl+Tab moves to the next poll. Each cell says which team it is for. The playoff committee and the hockey polls don't publish votes, so they have no Points column.

Football is the exception: NFL and NCAAF open a week rather than a day, and Alt+P and Alt+N move a week at a time. Weeks are named the way ESPN names them — Hall of Fame Weekend, Preseason Week 1, Week 1, Wild Card — and the arrows run from the first preseason week straight through to the Super Bowl, rolling over from one part of the season into the next.

Golf

PGA Tour and LPGA Tour open a dialog with the current tournament leaderboard and the full season schedule. Navigate between tabs with Tab or the arrow keys.

The Schedule tab lists every tournament this season. A finished one says Final with its winner and score, such as Chris Gotterup (-16); one being played says Live, and a cancelled one says so. Press Enter on any tournament to open its leaderboard: the dialog moves to the Leaderboard tab, and the window title and the line above the tabs name the tournament you are looking at. To go back to this week's, press Enter on it in the Schedule. If a leaderboard can't be loaded, F5 tries that tournament again.

World Cup

The 2026 FIFA World Cup and 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup each open a hub with four tabs:

  • Scores — match results by day (Alt+P / Alt+N to move between days)
  • Groups — group stage standings for all groups
  • Bracket — knockout round results, selectable by phase
  • News — latest headlines

NFL Draft

Select NFL Draft to browse picks by year and round.

Fantasy Cheatsheet

Open NFL and select Fantasy Cheatsheet for a fantasy football draft board: every active player ESPN ranks for fantasy — about 1,000 of them, including all 32 team defenses — with ESPN's consensus rank, average draft position, auction value, and projected season points. It is a starting point for your draft, not a live draft assistant.

The board is deliberately deeper than any league will draft, so late-round fliers and waiver targets are on it. Use the position and team filters, or the search box, to cut it down; sorting by ESPN rank keeps the players who go early at the top.

  • Search by player name or team abbreviation.
  • Position narrows the board to one position, to FLEX (RB, WR and TE together), to Rookies, or to rookies at a single position.
  • Team shows only one NFL team.
  • Sort by rank, ADP, auction value, projected points, or name.
  • Scoring switches between Standard, Half-PPR and PPR, and projected points change with it. ESPN publishes separate rankings for Standard and PPR only, so Half-PPR shows the PPR rankings. Nothing is re-downloaded.
  • Press Space on any player to mark them drafted or available. Check Hide drafted players to drop them off the board as your draft goes. Your marks are saved and are still there next time you open Scores.
  • Press Enter on a player for full details, including projected points in all three scoring formats.
  • Reload from ESPN downloads the board again — worth doing after a big injury or trade. Your drafted marks are kept.
  • Export to CSV saves the board to a spreadsheet file. It always writes every player, not just the ones the filters are showing, with both rank boards, ADP, auction value, the rookie flag, and projected points in all three scoring formats.

Kickers and team defenses show N/A for projected points. ESPN's published projections for those two positions are unusable, so Scores shows no number rather than a wrong one — their rank, ADP and auction value are still accurate.

Rookies are worked out from ESPN's own data: a player with no statistics from any earlier season. That catches undrafted rookies as well as drafted ones, but it would also flag a veteran who sat out a whole season, so treat it as a strong hint rather than a guarantee.

The Rank column is the player's position on this board — 1, 2, 3 with no gaps — in the order ESPN ranks them. ESPN's own published rank counts defensive players, punters and team-quarterback slots that no standard league drafts, so it skips large stretches; it is shown on the player details screen and in the CSV export if you want to compare against ESPN directly.

Most of the board shows N/A for average draft position. ESPN only publishes a real ADP for about 200 players — everyone else gets a placeholder value that would read as a genuine draft slot, so Scores shows nothing rather than something misleading. Those players sort to the bottom of an ADP sort, in rank order.

Favorites

Open any sport, then use the Team Hub button or press Alt+H to follow a team. Favorite teams appear as cards on the home screen with quick access to their schedule, roster, and news.

Each card is one item in the Favorite Teams list. After the team's name it has the team's last result, such as "Last: W vs LAD 2-1", then its next game with the date and time, such as "Next: @ VAN, 11/14/26, 11:45 AM", then two news headlines. While the team is playing, the last result gives way to the game in progress, with the score and the quarter, period or inning; in baseball it adds the pitcher (P), the batter (AB), the runners on base, the count and the outs. If ESPN isn't answering, the card says so; press F5 on the home screen to try again.

Settings

The Settings button on the home screen lets you control which sports appear in the list and in what order, and whether Scores checks for updates when it starts.

Known Issues

When closing the app, Windows may show a dialog warning that a temporary directory could not be removed. This is a known issue with the way the application is packaged and will be addressed in a future release. Clicking OK to dismiss the dialog is safe — no data is lost and the app has already closed normally.
